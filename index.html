Selected work

Auto-interp Labels Conflate Driver and Thermometer SAE Features

June 2026

A nineteen-experiment characterization of TopK SAE features in Pythia-160M layer 6. The driver/thermometer split between features auto-interp labels as "monosemantic" is a categorical property robust across intervention method and magnitude. Among features that pass sufficiency tests, only one third are true drivers when also tested for necessity.

Three headline findings
  1. 14 of 23 "monosemantic" features show zero drift toward their labeled concept when steered. Most are thermometers, not drivers.
  2. 2×2 necessity × sufficiency drops the TRUE driver rate from 56% to 33%. Single-direction patching miscategorizes ~40% of features.
  3. Same-labeled drivers route through different downstream attention heads. Causally validated via steering+ablation: mediator effects 30–44× larger than random-head ablation.
Solo work · $50 compute on a MacBook · full reproducible pipeline · paper retracts one of its own subsidiary claims under validation

Blog posts

  • SAE geometry, part two: measure the interactions, not the features July 2, 2026

    A follow-up. The first post killed five ways of measuring SAE feature function — all negative, all spectrum artifacts. This one asks the question that survives: a feature's function is how it combines with other features, not what it does alone, so I measured the network's actual feature interactions (the MLP's second-order coupling). Geometrically-close features genuinely interact more — and it is the only positive in the whole project to survive the spectrum-matched-random control that refuted everything else. But I over-reported it (+0.30); an adversarial pass (three skeptics + judge) caught that the honest rank-robust number is ~+0.16, and that a single global per-feature scalar out-predicts geometric neighbors ~2:1 at recovering a feature's true interaction partners. The answer: mostly global, with a thin but real local component — global-sufficient, local-insufficient. Code: feature-geometry-locality.

  • A random matrix debunked my SAE-geometry result. Every time. July 2, 2026

    Does SAE feature geometry reflect function (Sharkey et al.'s open problem — is it local or global)? I found a beautiful "local halo" — geometrically-close features looked functionally similar — then killed it five times, across three independent notions of function (direct effect, nonlinear on-distribution ablation, co-firing). Each time a spectrum-matched random matrix reproduced the halo bin-for-bin: the signal was measuring the proxy's spectrum, not the network. The reusable lesson: never headline a geometry→function statistic without a spectrum-matched null — raw R² and the intuitive label-permutation null are both beaten by any fixed linear map. Plus the mechanism behind the recurring confound (a shared confidence-collapse direction that promotes glitch tokens). Code: feature-geometry-locality.

  • Read early, steer late: a concept is detectable at layer 0 but only controllable deep June 30, 2026

    Where does the read≠cause split live across a model's depth? For four concepts I measured the layer at which each becomes readable (decodable) vs steerable (intervening changes the output). The gap is large and consistent: every concept is perfectly readable at layer 0 (it's in the words) but only steerable at layers 15–18 (near where the model commits to an answer). Detection is lexical and immediate; control is deep and late — so decodability at a layer does not imply control there. Plus a sub-investigation locating "certainty" at ~layer 15 (weakly), and a tidy "abstraction = depth" prediction that the data refuted. Built with FeatureScope.

  • Compositional but not a direction: when the model's reasoning lives in attention, not a vector June 23, 2026

    The capstone — the operator type that breaks the "concepts are directions" paradigm. Comparison ("A better than B") genuinely binds the role (winner flips +2.31 when you flip better↔worse, entities frozen) — but it is not a direction: the role-vector built exactly like the negation driver has reusability cosine 0.02 (vs 0.92) and steering never flips the winner (0% at k=8). Predicted from first principles: a position-agnostic vector can't encode which entity wins, so the model must do it by attention. Completes the typology (negation/intensification/conjunction = directions; comparison = attention-routing) and marks a real limit of the probing-and-steering toolkit: relational reasoning lives in circuits, not vectors.

  • The model's compositional toolbox: averaging, amplifying, and a negation that won't cancel June 23, 2026

    Does the model do algebra on concepts? Tested four composition operators on Gemma-2-2b sentiment: conjunction = clean averaging (judge(A and B) ≈ mean of parts); intensification = symmetric gain (extremely good/bad both amplify); negation = a reusable causal flip/driver (cos 0.92, steering flips judgments); double-negation = broken — "not not good" saturates and won't return to positive. No single law: a heterogeneous toolbox, some operators clean and roughly linear, one outright broken. The seductive "one asymmetry governs everything" hypothesis died under a clean test — the most useful thing that happened.

  • The driver map: finding what a model thinks with — and catching the tool when it goes blind June 23, 2026

    The engineering companion: which of a concept's thousands of features does the model actually think with? You can't read it off — you measure cause (steer/ablate). Mapping read-score vs cause-score over 9,037 Gemma features: correlation ≈ 0 — most readable features are thermometers, not drivers (replicating the project's first Pythia finding). And a live lesson in instrument-blindness: the cheap tool (attribution patching) caught 3 of 4 steering-confirmed drivers but went blind on one (a real driver buried at rank 1298) — exactly the LayerNorm/saturation false-negative predicted for a residual-stream SAE at mid-depth. To find what a model thinks with, measure causation — and check the causation tool against a real intervention.

  • Drivers and thermometers: what a model represents vs what it thinks with June 22, 2026

    The unifying result. From first principles: a concept is a direction, and a real one should both read (decode) and write (steer). Tested on Gemma-2-2b: sentiment reads and writes (a clean steering knob, swing ~2.9) — a driver; truth reads just as well (~0.66 AUROC) but barely writes (~0) — a thermometer. The model reasons with concepts but only carries facts. This single read≠write axis unifies the whole arc (trust, latent knowledge, the factual boundary) and explains confident hallucination and why fact-editing doesn't stick. Honest 2B caveat with a falsifiable scale prediction: truth should turn from thermometer to driver in bigger models.

  • When a model is wrong, does it already know better? Recovering suppressed answers from transfer-stable features June 22, 2026

    An eliciting-latent-knowledge result. When this sentiment model is wrong out-of-distribution, is the right answer still inside it, suppressed? Using the transfer-stable feature subspace as a latent-knowledge probe: across 897 OOD errors, the robust core recovers the correct answer 61.5% of the time (CI 58–65%) vs 49% for random features — and ~69% on near-chance financial headlines. So most OOD errors are readout corruption, not ignorance: spurious topic features override genuine concept features, but the correct computation is still linearly decodable. The model already knew.

  • What makes an out-of-distribution answer untrustworthy — and when interpretability can tell June 22, 2026

    The synthesis: after four fleets (invent, attack, self-diagnose, prove) and a genuinely harder shift (tweets, poems, near-chance financial headlines), the account that survives is "trust a prediction to the degree transfer-stable features carry it." Confidence collapses under shift (proven rank-invariance + correlation +0.87 with accuracy); a single-probe reliable-evidence attribution beats it decisively (P=1.0) and most where the model is weakest — its edge doubles as accuracy falls. The mechanism is verified (the reliable sub-probe is a better OOD oracle, 0.72→0.82; wrong calls have reliable features objecting). And two of my own overclaims are corrected: density does work in an extrapolation regime (counterexample AUROC 0.81), and the mechanism is "better oracle," not "independent failure." Every number reproduced by me.

  • Placing a result: where "interpretability beats confidence" sits in the literature — and the paper that's its biggest threat June 21, 2026

    Before building on the agreement-signal result, I did what a finding that size deserves: reproduced it from scratch as a saved script (a review of my own work caught that it had never actually been saved — only run inline), then read the field hard enough to place it. It sits in an empty cell between disagreement-for-reliability (Agreement-on-the-Line) and interpretability-native abstention (Ferrando et al.'s "Do I Know This Entity?"). The biggest threat is Kantamneni et al.'s "Are SAEs Useful?" — SAEs don't beat baselines under covariate shift — so I narrow the claim to selective risk not accuracy, switch to the AUGRC metric, and pre-commit to reporting a null. Mostly related work and honesty, not new wins.

  • When interpretability beats confidence: distrust the answer your trustworthy self rejects June 21, 2026

    The capstone ended on a catch — a detector could tell when it was wrong OOD, but plain confidence did the work and the interpretability signal failed. This is the sharpened version that flips it: distrust a prediction when the full detector disagrees with the version of itself that uses only its trustworthy (transfer-stable) features. It beats plain confidence at knowing-when-wrong on all six conditions (3 domains × 2 poolings, paired P≈1.0) — and a control (random-feature sub-models, which only tie confidence) proves the interpretability is load-bearing. The first verified case in the program where an interpretability-native method beats the simple baseline. Updated + corrected: a 59-agent search finds a graded refinement (reliable-code surprisal, AUROC 0.73→0.83); a follow-up self-audit then caught real mistakes in my own evaluation — the "6/6, P≈1.0" framing overstated power (the poolings are correlated, so n≈3 domains), the AUGRC metric was biased toward the binary signal, and the precise dividing line is two-view class-relative contrast, not "disagreement." The core claim survives domain-clustered significance; the overclaim was in the evidence's strength, not its direction.

  • Trustworthy not by being right, but by knowing when it's wrong June 21, 2026

    Capstone. Every attempt to make an SAE-feature detector more accurate out-of-distribution failed (de-spuriousing, a generalization predictor, sign-stability, multi-domain training — eleven methods, no reliable win). So I reframed: a trustworthy detector isn't one that's always right, it's one that knows when it's wrong and abstains. That works — abstaining on the least-confident half raises OOD accuracy ~0.73→0.83 across three held-out domains, verified vs random abstention. Honest catch: the working signal is plain classifier confidence (selective prediction), not the interpretability-native one I hoped for, which failed at chance.

  • Removing spurious features makes a semantic detector generalize June 20, 2026

    Semantic detectors built from SAE features don't transfer across distributions — a sentiment probe trained on movie reviews scores 0.66 on product reviews because it partly learns domain vocabulary. Dropping the domain-spurious features raises cross-distribution F1 to ~0.73 (verified leak-free, ~9σ over a random control). But the follow-up deflated the headline: a better base recipe (max-pooling + selection) reaches 0.77 without de-spuriousing, and the levers don't compound — so de-spuriousing rescues a weak detector, while recipe choice is the bigger lever. Both the result and its correction are kept visible, same standard as the prior post's retraction.

  • Detecting and steering SAE features: where it works, where it breaks June 20, 2026

    A 60-agent search for the best cross-distribution detector recipe (L1-select + MLP; the optimal feature count scales with SAE width — 100 on a 2k SAE, 400 on Gemma Scope's 16k), then steering along feature directions. The detector finding holds: surface properties are far more tractable than semantic/safety ones. Includes a retraction — a "causal direction beats probe direction for detox" result I first published fell apart under rigorous measurement (real classifiers, 288 sampled generations). The rerun caught my own weak-metric artifact, which is exactly the standard this project holds the field to.

  • Are SAE features real? Stop certifying features, validate detectors June 17, 2026

    Individual SAE features mostly can't be certified "real" — only ~1–2% replicate across seeds, and "realness" splits into independent axes (stability, causation, interpretability) that disagree. The trustworthy unit is a validated detector: a sparse feature combination judged by held-out and cross-distribution task performance. A recipe search (L1-select + MLP) reaches ~0.98 cross-distribution F1 on surface concepts on a real Gemma Scope SAE; semantic concepts improve to ~0.82 but aren't cleanly robust. Positions the work against SAEBench and Paulo & Belrose.

  • Efficient AtP*: 30× speedup for SAE circuit discovery June 3, 2026

    Built the diagnostic to find why attribution patching fails at deep layers in Pythia-160M. The answer: attention softmax saturation, exclusively (GELU and LayerNorm aren't responsible). Then built the fix — closed-form softmax-aware AtP — which achieves Pearson 0.993 with full activation patching at 2 model passes per feature-position. Same cost as plain AtP, 5× lower error, 30× speedup over AP.

  • Attribution patching breaks at depth: a layer-adaptive fix June 2, 2026

    Attribution patching is the standard cheap approximation for SAE circuit discovery. On 180 (feature, head) pairs in Pythia-160M, its agreement with activation patching drops from Pearson 0.999 at the first downstream layer to 0.78 at the fifth. Integrated gradients (the Marks et al. 2024 default) is worse than AtP at early layers. A one-line layer-adaptive method recovers Pearson 0.99 with full AP at 23% of the compute — a 5× speedup.
