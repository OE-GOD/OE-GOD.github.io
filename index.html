Selected work

Auto-interp Labels Conflate Driver and Thermometer SAE Features

June 2026

A nineteen-experiment characterization of TopK SAE features in Pythia-160M layer 6. The driver/thermometer split between features auto-interp labels as "monosemantic" is a categorical property robust across intervention method and magnitude. Among features that pass sufficiency tests, only one third are true drivers when also tested for necessity.

Three headline findings
  1. 14 of 23 "monosemantic" features show zero drift toward their labeled concept when steered. Most are thermometers, not drivers.
  2. 2×2 necessity × sufficiency drops the TRUE driver rate from 56% to 33%. Single-direction patching miscategorizes ~40% of features.
  3. Same-labeled drivers route through different downstream attention heads. Causally validated via steering+ablation: mediator effects 30–44× larger than random-head ablation.
Solo work · $50 compute on a MacBook · full reproducible pipeline · paper retracts one of its own subsidiary claims under validation

Blog posts

  • Trustworthy not by being right, but by knowing when it's wrong June 21, 2026

    Capstone. Every attempt to make an SAE-feature detector more accurate out-of-distribution failed (de-spuriousing, a generalization predictor, sign-stability, multi-domain training — eleven methods, no reliable win). So I reframed: a trustworthy detector isn't one that's always right, it's one that knows when it's wrong and abstains. That works — abstaining on the least-confident half raises OOD accuracy ~0.73→0.83 across three held-out domains, verified vs random abstention. Honest catch: the working signal is plain classifier confidence (selective prediction), not the interpretability-native one I hoped for, which failed at chance.

  • Removing spurious features makes a semantic detector generalize June 20, 2026

    Semantic detectors built from SAE features don't transfer across distributions — a sentiment probe trained on movie reviews scores 0.66 on product reviews because it partly learns domain vocabulary. Dropping the domain-spurious features raises cross-distribution F1 to ~0.73 (verified leak-free, ~9σ over a random control). But the follow-up deflated the headline: a better base recipe (max-pooling + selection) reaches 0.77 without de-spuriousing, and the levers don't compound — so de-spuriousing rescues a weak detector, while recipe choice is the bigger lever. Both the result and its correction are kept visible, same standard as the prior post's retraction.

  • Detecting and steering SAE features: where it works, where it breaks June 20, 2026

    A 60-agent search for the best cross-distribution detector recipe (L1-select + MLP; the optimal feature count scales with SAE width — 100 on a 2k SAE, 400 on Gemma Scope's 16k), then steering along feature directions. The detector finding holds: surface properties are far more tractable than semantic/safety ones. Includes a retraction — a "causal direction beats probe direction for detox" result I first published fell apart under rigorous measurement (real classifiers, 288 sampled generations). The rerun caught my own weak-metric artifact, which is exactly the standard this project holds the field to.

  • Are SAE features real? Stop certifying features, validate detectors June 17, 2026

    Individual SAE features mostly can't be certified "real" — only ~1–2% replicate across seeds, and "realness" splits into independent axes (stability, causation, interpretability) that disagree. The trustworthy unit is a validated detector: a sparse feature combination judged by held-out and cross-distribution task performance. A recipe search (L1-select + MLP) reaches ~0.98 cross-distribution F1 on surface concepts on a real Gemma Scope SAE; semantic concepts improve to ~0.82 but aren't cleanly robust. Positions the work against SAEBench and Paulo & Belrose.

  • Efficient AtP*: 30× speedup for SAE circuit discovery June 3, 2026

    Built the diagnostic to find why attribution patching fails at deep layers in Pythia-160M. The answer: attention softmax saturation, exclusively (GELU and LayerNorm aren't responsible). Then built the fix — closed-form softmax-aware AtP — which achieves Pearson 0.993 with full activation patching at 2 model passes per feature-position. Same cost as plain AtP, 5× lower error, 30× speedup over AP.

  • Attribution patching breaks at depth: a layer-adaptive fix June 2, 2026

    Attribution patching is the standard cheap approximation for SAE circuit discovery. On 180 (feature, head) pairs in Pythia-160M, its agreement with activation patching drops from Pearson 0.999 at the first downstream layer to 0.78 at the fifth. Integrated gradients (the Marks et al. 2024 default) is worse than AtP at early layers. A one-line layer-adaptive method recovers Pearson 0.99 with full AP at 23% of the compute — a 5× speedup.
