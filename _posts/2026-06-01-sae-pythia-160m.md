# Auto-interp Labels Conflate Driver and Thermometer SAE Features

*A case study on Pythia-160M layer 6 — and a cheap test you can run on any SAE.*

*June 2026 · [Paper PDF](https://github.com/OE-GOD/sae-pythia-160m/blob/main/writeup/sae_pythia_layer6_paper.pdf) · [Reproducible code](https://github.com/OE-GOD/sae-pythia-160m)*

---

## The headline

I trained a TopK sparse autoencoder on Pythia-160M's residual stream at layer 6 (16,384 features, k=64), then ran seventeen experiments to characterize what the features causally do — not just what they fire on.

Three findings I think the field should know about:

**1. Most "monosemantic" auto-interp features are thermometers, not causal drivers.** Across 23 high-confidence monosemantic features whose auto-interp labels could be mapped to concept tokens, **14 (60.9%) produced zero predicted-concept tokens when steered.** Only 4 (17.4%) were clear drivers. The distribution is sharply bimodal — features are categorically *drivers* or *thermometers*, not on a continuum.

**2. Single-direction patching systematically over-counts drivers.** Adding noising (necessity test) to denoising (sufficiency test) — the 2×2 framework recommended by Heimersheim & Nanda (2024) — drops the TRUE driver rate from 56% to 33% on a 9-feature subset. Two features previously classified as drivers turn out to be OR-circuit components (sufficient but redundant — the model has backups). One feature previously classified as a thermometer is revealed to be an AND-circuit component (necessary but not sufficient alone). About 40% of features get miscategorized by single-direction patching.

**3. Same-labeled drivers route through different downstream attention heads.** Per-head path patching applied to the 3 TRUE drivers: f10047 and f13131 (both "newline" features) are mediated primarily through L8H10; f15245 (also "newline") is mediated through L8H9. Causal validation via steering+ablation: ablating the identified mediator drops concept prediction **30-44× more than ablating a random control head**. The auto-interp label "Newline tokens" collapses meaningful mechanistic differences between drivers.

---

## Why I think this matters

The mech-interp community is increasingly using SAE features for safety-relevant applications: monitoring features labeled "deception," "harmful intent," etc. If a "deception feature" turns out to be a thermometer (fires when the model is being deceptive, but isn't part of the deception circuit), monitoring it doesn't help you intervene. Discriminating drivers from thermometers — and identifying which downstream heads route their effects — is load-bearing for the safety use case.

The good news: the tests are cheap.
- Logit weight analysis (`decoder_col @ W_U` for concept tokens) → milliseconds per feature
- Sufficiency test (steering or SAE-feature patching) → ~10 seconds per feature
- Necessity test (noising) → ~5 seconds per feature
- Per-head path patching → ~5 seconds per feature × downstream head

The bad news: most SAE papers (mine included, until I ran these) don't include them.

## The driver/thermometer split — first the small sample

Several auto-interp features were labeled "Newlines." When I tested them via residual-stream steering at α = peak activation, the results split sharply:

| Feature | Logit weight for newlines | Newlines per 30 steered tokens |
|---|---|---|
| **f2255** | **0.494** | **19** |
| f2757 | 0.489 | 2 |
| f15245 | 0.477 | 0 |
| **f6767** | **0.042** | **0** |

f2255 and f6767 both fire on newline-containing tokens. Both passed auto-interp's monosemantic classification. Nearly identical projection onto the cluster's PC1 (0.52 each). But one drives newlines causally and one doesn't.

**Logit weight cleanly explains why.** f2255's decoder direction has substantial positive projection onto newline output tokens; f6767's doesn't. Auto-interp labels what makes a feature fire (the input pattern). Logit weight reveals what the feature pushes for in the output. **The two diverge.**

## At scale — most features are thermometers

I extended the test to all 23 high-confidence monosemantic features whose labels mapped cleanly to concept tokens.

| Verdict | Count | % |
|---|---|---|
| Driver (Δ ≥ 3 concept tokens per 30 steered) | 4 | 17.4% |
| Thermometer (Δ ≤ 0.5) | 18 | 78.3% |
| Ambiguous | 1 | 4.3% |

**The distribution is bimodal.** 14/23 features (60.9%) have Δ = exactly 0. Features are categorically drivers or thermometers, not on a continuous spectrum.

The qualitative finding is robust to threshold choice — thermometer-majority holds across 10/10 tested thresholds.

---

## Method robustness — three intervention protocols

A natural objection: maybe steering is the wrong intervention method. I compared three:

| Method | Driver rate | n |
|---|---|---|
| Steering (synthetic α × W_dec) | 17% | 23 |
| Whole-residual patching | 87% | 23 |
| SAE-feature patching | 56% | 9 |

Methods disagree on borderline features, but the *categorical* distinction holds: features that drive their labeled concept do so robustly under multiple methods; features that don't, don't — even when amplified to 30× their natural firing magnitude.

![Three-way comparison](/assets/figures/fig2_three_way_comparison.png)

The whole-residual patching number (87%) is suspiciously high — and that's the OR-gate trap I'll come back to.

---

## Magnitude sweep — categorical, not magnitude-dependent

What if thermometers are just drivers operating at too-small magnitudes? I swept intervention magnitude from 0× to 30× the natural firing magnitude.

![Magnitude sweep](/assets/figures/fig1_magnitude_sweep.png)

Sharply categorical:
- **5 newline-cluster features** show rocket-shaped curves. Concept-logit-diff grows from 1–8 nats at 1× to 200–360 nats at 30×.
- **4 non-newline labeled features** stay flat. Under ±5 nats across all magnitudes tested.

**Amplifying a thermometer does not make it a driver.** It makes it a slightly noisier thermometer. The driver/thermometer property is intrinsic to the feature's relationship with the predicted concept, not an artifact of how hard we're pushing.

---

## The 2×2 — necessity AND sufficiency

This is the methodological centerpiece. The protocol all three single-method experiments above test is **sufficiency**: does activating the feature alone produce the concept? Heimersheim & Nanda (2024) note that sufficiency and necessity can give very different answers about the same circuit:

- **AND-circuit** (component is necessary alongside teammates): denoising/sufficiency misses it because remaining teammates are corrupt.
- **OR-circuit** (component is sufficient but redundant): denoising/sufficiency over-counts it because the model has backups.

I complemented SAE-feature denoising with SAE-feature **noising** on the same 9 features. For each clean firing position, subtract `f_X * decoder_col_X` from the residual stream and measure the drop in `log P(actual next token)`.

A gotcha I hit and want to flag: my first attempt used `mean(logit)` over the concept-token set as the metric. This produced uninterpretable *negative* drops — i.e., ablation appeared to *increase* concept-logit. Diagnosis: ablation pushed the residual stream out of distribution, the model's output collapsed toward uniform, and the *mean* over many low-frequency concept tokens rose without specific improvement. This is exactly the "breaking the model" false positive Heimersheim & Nanda warn about. Switching to `logprob(actual next token)` — a normalized probability of a specific token — eliminated it.

The 2×2 result:

![2x2 classification](/assets/figures/fig4_two_by_two_classification.png)

|  | **Sufficient (denoising = driver)** | **Not sufficient (denoising = thermometer)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Necessary** (noising) | TRUE DRIVER: 3 | AND-circuit component: 1 |
| **Not necessary** (noising) | OR-circuit component: 2 | THERMOMETER: 1 |

Plus 2 ambiguous. **TRUE driver rate = 33% (3/9)** — substantially below the 56% from sufficiency alone. Two newline features previously counted as drivers (f11488, f13821) turn out to be OR-circuit components; one decimal-numerical feature previously counted as thermometer (f1989) turns out to be an AND-circuit component.

**Single-direction patching miscategorizes ~40% of features tested.**

## Per-head path patching — same labels, different pathways

For each TRUE driver, I asked: which downstream attention heads consume this feature's contribution? Following the IOI methodology (Wang et al. 2023), I path-patched per-head: for each (layer L, head h) with L > 6, I subtracted the feature's contribution from THAT head's q/k/v input only, leaving every other head's input unperturbed.

![Per-head path patching](/assets/figures/fig5_path_patching_heatmaps.png)

Each driver has a clearly concentrated mediator:

| Feature | Auto-interp label | Primary positive mediator |
|---|---|---|
| f10047 | Newline (CSS/HTML) | L8H10 |
| f13131 | Newline (general) | L8H10 |
| f15245 | Newline (XML/HTML) | L8H9 |

f10047 and f13131 share L8H10; f15245 instead routes primarily through L8H9. **Auto-interp labels them all "Newline tokens" but they are not mechanistically interchangeable.**

---

## Causal validation — and where I had to walk back a claim

Looking at the heatmaps, I noticed something striking. f10047 has L8H10 as a *positive* mediator and L8H9 as a *negative* one. f15245 has the opposite pattern: L8H9 positive, L8H10 negative. The same head has opposite-sign effects on the two features.

In the first version of this writeup, I called these "negative mediator heads" and drew an analogy to the **Negative Name Mover Heads** of the IOI circuit — heads that systematically write in the *opposite* direction of the correct answer, possibly implementing some kind of hedging or competitive dynamic.

I tested this claim with a steering-plus-ablation protocol. For each (feature, mediator_head, expected_direction) pair, steer the feature at 3× peak magnitude, then either ablate the target mediator's z output or ablate a random control head (averaged over 15 controls). The *selectivity* of the mediator was its effect minus the random-control effect.

Predictions:
- Positive mediator → ablating reduces concept logprob → selectivity > 0
- Negative mediator → ablating *increases* concept logprob (the suppressor is gone) → selectivity < 0

Results:

| Feature | Target head | Predicted | Mediator contr. | Random contr. | Selectivity | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| f10047 | L8H10 | positive | +0.108 | +0.003 | **+0.105** | ✓✓ (35× random) |
| f13131 | L8H10 | positive | +0.239 | +0.008 | **+0.232** | ✓✓ (32× random) |
| f15245 | L8H9 | positive | +0.410 | +0.009 | **+0.401** | ✓✓ (44× random) |
| f10047 | L8H9 | negative | +0.012 | −0.010 | +0.022 | ✗ |
| f15245 | L8H10 | negative | +0.258 | +0.062 | +0.196 | ✗ |

**Positive mediators robustly validated** — all three show selectivity 30-44× larger than random-head ablation. Path patching identifies real causal mediators, not artifacts.

**Negative-effect heads do NOT validate as suppressors.** The two heads I'd called "negative mediators" don't behave as predicted under steering ablation. Their mediator contributions are positive or neutral, not negative. The Negative Name Mover Heads analogy I'd reached for was overstated.

I retracted it. The negative effects in path patching are real, but they don't correspond to inhibitory roles in the same way IOI's suppressor heads do. Two-protocol triangulation (path patching + steering+ablation) caught this. **This is exactly the kind of overinterpretation Heimersheim & Nanda warn about and exactly why running both protocols matters.**

## What I learned from doing this

Three things I didn't expect:

**Auto-interp is more wrong than I expected.** Even high-confidence "monosemantic" labels collapse mechanistically distinct features. 60.9% of confidently-monosemantic features have *no* causal effect on their labeled concept under steering. I went into this thinking the gap would be 10-20%; it's more like 60%.

**Methodology choice changes the answer.** The same 9 features get different driver rates depending on whether you test sufficiency, necessity, or both. 56% becomes 33%. Different intervention protocols are not interchangeable. The community treats them as if they are.

**The "breaking the model" false positive is easy to walk into.** I hit it twice in this project — once on the noising metric (had to switch from mean-logit to logprob-of-specific-token), and once on the negative-mediator interpretation (had to retract). Both times, normalized metrics + multi-protocol triangulation caught it. Without those, I would have published wrong claims.

---

## Honest limitations

- **Small model, single layer.** Pythia-160M, layer 6. Findings may not generalize to Gemma 2 or larger.
- **Small samples.** n=23 for the population work, n=9 for the deep dives. Population breakdowns are illustrative, not statistically tight.
- **Auto-interp not human-validated.** Used Kimi K2 labels at face value.
- **Solo, not peer-reviewed.** Take the conclusions seriously but check them yourself before citing.
- **Single feature class for the deep dives.** Most of the 9-feature analyses lean on newline features. Other feature categories (citations, math, file paths) tested at smaller n.
